# 参考图视觉系统

这些参数是对用户提供的五张封面参考的视觉归纳，不是对图片文字的复述。

## Shared subject treatment

- The subject is a real, dimensional portrait cutout. Preserve facial identity, natural skin texture, hair wisps, hand anatomy, clothing weave, and the original gesture.
- Use a clean white or warm-white sticker edge around the cutout, usually 8–18 px at 1080 px width, with a soft contact shadow. The edge follows hair and fingers rather than becoming a crude rectangle.
- The photo remains the visual anchor. The title may overlap the surrounding empty area or a paper strip, but never the eyes, face, fingers, or key product.
- Keep perspective and light consistent: paper layers cast small soft shadows, icons sit on paper, and foreground subject edges have a believable separation from the background.

## Family A: yellow-orange collage

- Base: warm cream or aged paper, approximately `#F7EFE2` and `#E7D7C4`.
- Accent: lemon yellow `#F8D447`, golden yellow `#F6C52F`, orange `#F36A1D`, warm brown `#87502F`.
- Text: near-black `#080808`, often on torn yellow or cream paper; occasional white lettering on orange.
- Texture: torn paper fibers, newspaper/type fragments, notebook traces, hand-brushed yellow patches, white ripped borders, uneven ink density.
- Decoration: light bulb, speech bubble, star, exclamation mark, hand-drawn underline, short radiating lines, arrow scribbles. Use a few elements with clear spacing.
- Layout: for 9:16, put the person lower-left or lower-center and stack title strips in the upper two-thirds. For 16:9, put the person on the left and title strips on the right.

## Family B: blue-mint collage

- Base: warm paper `#F7F2E9`, pale beige `#DED0C2`, or light grid paper.
- Accent: royal blue `#0B3DC4` to `#164BD2`, mint `#A9DCCA`, teal `#4FC2B0`, plus white and charcoal.
- Text: black on mint/cream strips, or white on a saturated blue strip. Use one dominant black/white title contrast per block.
- Texture: torn blue paint, mint brush patches, notebook and grid fragments, white torn edges, slightly uneven hand ink.
- Decoration: megaphone, phone, laptop, checkboxes, chat bubbles, arrows, stars, small handwritten English or Chinese notes only when useful. Icons are simple line drawings, not pasted app screenshots.
- Layout: keep title blocks to the upper or right side; place the person lower-left or left-center. A bottom torn note can hold a short call to action, but never a paragraph.

## Family C: yellow-black office-demo

- Background: a real softly blurred office, desk, laptop, or work scene from the input photo or a compatible edit. Keep depth and lighting photographic.
- Accent: yellow `#FFD21F`, black `#0A0A0A`, white `#FFFFFF`, with small warm gray shadows.
- Text: heavy geometric sans or block display Chinese, yellow and white on black or black on yellow. Use a thick black 3D-like offset shadow/outline for separation; this family does not need the torn-paper white sticker border used by the collage families.
- Layout: subject occupies one side, product or phone is held toward camera with correct perspective, and title sits in the empty upper/right area. One yellow highlight bar can anchor a subline.
- Decoration: very limited yellow emphasis strokes or arrows; no collage overload and no fake UI panel surrounding the person.

## Type and hierarchy

- Main title: very heavy, hand-brushed or chunky display Chinese, compact line spacing, imperfect ink edges. It should feel written with a broad marker or brush, not like a thin web font.
- Outline: white sticker border around title strips or individual text groups; add a restrained black shadow for separation. The outline should not erase the brush texture.
- Use one dominant title, one supporting line, and at most one small label. Keep words large enough to read in a fast-scrolling feed.
- Punctuation can be retained when it is part of the user's exact title, especially `？` and `！`; do not add punctuation just for decoration.
- Do not invent the reference images' English slogans, checklists, comment prompts, dates, episode labels, or extra claims. Add secondary copy only when the user supplies it.
